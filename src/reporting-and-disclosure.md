# Reporting and Disclosure

## Research decision and learning outcome

- Decide whether the evidence supports a private report, requires more work or refutes the claim.
- Produce a useful draft only when justified. A supported hold or rejection is a successful outcome.
- Help a maintainer reproduce behavior, understand demonstrated impact and assess a fix.
- An agent drafts; a human judges and sends. No exercise sends or publishes anything.

## Credible baseline and controlled change

- Baseline: an ordinary agent assessment and draft from the same complete fieldwork notes and source records.
- Add the explicit readiness, verification and channel workflow—not extra evidence or withheld caveats.
- Keep model, task, evidence access, permissions and budget the same. Submission tools remain unavailable in both conditions.
- Use synthetic or reviewed public material, separate expected decisions, and fresh sessions. Do not use confidential client findings.

## Demonstration outline

- Predict report, hold or refute. Preserve the baseline decision and its evidence.
- Repeat through the workflow below; inspect factual support rather than polish or length.
- Change one material fact, then reassess whether a report is still justified.
- This comparison is planned; no controlled workflow-benefit result is claimed.

## 1. Decide whether the evidence is ready

- Confidence means how well the claim survives questions; severity means demonstrated impact.
- High: mechanism/callers, reproduction/control, reachability and impact established.
- Medium: solid reproduction with only a nonmaterial inference, stated clearly.
- Blocked: a missing answer could change the claim; save the question and next step instead of a report.
- Read full functions/callers; test a one-variable control. Record actual outcomes and attacker capabilities.
- Name the expected objection and weakest link. Holds and refuted results are useful too.

## 2. Verify the current project and private route

- Authorized live checks immediately before drafting: supported release, exact source, reproduction and realistic configuration.
- Distinguish fork from upstream; do not invent affected ranges or patched versions.
- Check duplicates, severity and proposed remediation.
- Use upstream's policy/required fields and confirmed private channel; a fork's policy or signed-out redirect is insufficient.
- No route/network access: hold and ask, not a public fallback or policy bypass.
- Save dates/sources; completed checkboxes do not prove work occurred.

## 3. Collect the inputs

- One target/finding, tested version, evidence, limits and observed impact.
- Separate observed facts, inferences and unanswered questions.
- Severity/vector or unscored reason; applicable CWE or a reason why none fits.
- Operator-supplied identity/contact, policy, coordination window, credit and CVE stance; ask rather than invent.
- Keep raw sensitive evidence private and review anything shared.

## 4. Draft for the actual channel

- For an advisory form: separate local fenced fields for title, severity, weaknesses, affected versions, confirmed fixes/unknown and description.
- Fences must contain any inner reproduction fences safely.
- Description outline:
  - Affected version; who can do what to whom and under which conditions.
  - Source/callers and the failed boundary.
  - Reproduction, expected/actual outcomes and control.
  - Observed impact, preconditions, limits and weakest link.
  - Recovery if applicable; remediation options and what was actually tested.
  - Post-fix attack/control/legitimate-use checks.
  - Related advisory distinction if needed.
  - Supplied coordination, credit and CVE agreements.
- Preserve material caveats and a protected limitation sentence.
- No bundled independent findings; no renderer/submission tool supplied here.

## 5. Review, then let the human send

- Removable sender notes: route/policy/date, evidence/release/lines, confidence, objections and severity alternatives.
- Review actual evidence, attribution, impact, redaction and policy—not only a checklist or hash.
- Human approval is not automatic submission; drafting agents get no send/publication tools.

## Counterexamples and evaluation

- Missing control: a plausible mechanism is not a complete reproduction.
- Fixed current release: an old observation does not establish a current affected version.
- Fork policy, unknown route, missing values or changed evidence: stop rather than invent support or use a public fallback.
- Score readiness decisions, affected-version accuracy, reproduction/control detail, supported impact, redaction and a usable maintainer handoff.
- Count unsupported claims and invented agreements as errors; do not reward a polished but unjustified report.
- Record calls, elapsed time, available token/cache usage and human review effort. A short direct draft may be enough for a complete, simple case.
- A larger workflow adds maintenance and can still miss a material assumption. Check actual evidence, not only its checklist.

## Learner checkpoint and evidence still needed

- Give an unfamiliar case a report/hold/refute decision, supporting evidence, a limitation and one next action.
- Draft only if the case supports it. Explain which new fact would change a hold or rejection.
- Further tests need authorization. Synthetic material is **training-only / do not submit**; offline examples are not live verification.
- Verify expected decisions independently and preserve paired runs before claiming workflow or learner benefit.

## Real follow-up stays human-owned

- After an actual human send, record the supplied date/channel, agreed window, replies and next action privately.
- Keep prior agreements. Draft creation starts no clock; window expiry does not trigger publication.
